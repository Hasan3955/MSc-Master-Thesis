# MSc-Master-Thesis
Evaluating AI Detection Models for Social Media Content

Management Summary
This thesis systematically evaluated the effectiveness of widely used AI detection tools (Grammarly, GPTZero, and ZeroGPT) in identifying AI-generated content on social media platforms like X (formerly Twitter), where posts are brief and linguistically informal. The evaluation combined a benchmarking study using 1,600 tweets generated by various AI models (GPT-2, GPT-3.5, GPT-4o) and human-authored content with in-depth qualitative insights from expert interviews.
Key findings revealed that, contrary to initial assumptions, more sophisticated generative AI models such as GPT-4o were easier for current detection tools to identify compared to earlier models like GPT-2. While older models produce less coherent text, they also exhibit erratic or unnatural linguistic patterns that overlap with low-quality human writing, complicating classification. In contrast, advanced models exhibit distinctive and consistent patterns, which detection methodologies, particularly transformer-based and hybrid approaches, can exploit effectively (Zellers et al., 2019; Solaiman et al., 2019). GPTZero and ZeroGPT, which utilize advanced, multi-faceted detection frameworks, consistently outperformed Grammarly, which leans more on statistical heuristics (Elkhatat et al., 2023).
Qualitative insights from expert interviews provided critical context, revealing widespread skepticism about the long-term viability of AI detectors. Experts emphasized that the detection landscape is constrained by the "arms race" dynamic: each advancement in generative AI reduces the reliability of existing detection tools. Ethical concerns such as the risk of false positives in academic or journalistic contexts and privacy limitations related to data access and behavioral tracking were identified as key barriers (Glikson & Woolley, 2020; Elkhatat et al., 2023). Furthermore, there is increasing concern over the feedback loop where detectors train on data increasingly influenced by generative AI, further complicating accuracy.
Based on these findings, several practical recommendations were made. These include the adoption of hybrid and modular detection frameworks that integrate linguistic, syntactic, and deep learning techniques (Fagni et al., 2021; Kumarage et al., 2023), especially for use on platforms where short-form content dominates. Tools should incorporate metadata and behavioral signals, such as posting patterns and device data to enhance contextual understanding and reliability (Rossi et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024). Ensuring transparency through explainable AI (Rai, 2019), retraining models regularly to counter concept drift (Akram, 2021; Weber-Wulff et al., 2023), and promoting collaboration among academic, industrial, and regulatory stakeholders were also highlighted as critical.
In conclusion, while current AI detection tools demonstrate measurable effectiveness against certain models, their long-term reliability is uncertain due to the relentless progression of generative AI. Future-proof moderation strategies must remain adaptive, ethically grounded, and multi-dimensional recognizing that detection success today does not ensure success tomorrow.
